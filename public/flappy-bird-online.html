What you are actually playing

Beakdown is an original arcade game, not a rework of the one in your search bar. You fly by flapping, and you fight by flying: when two birds collide, the higher one wins, so every enemy is a duel decided by altitude. Around that one rule sits a whole run: eggs to snatch before they hatch into fresh enemies, a star that briefly turns you from prey into predator, and waves that keep escalating while the ground changes beneath you. There are no pipes and no corridor.

How Beakdown works

The one rule

Every clash compares height. The bird whose crest is higher when two bodies meet wins, and the loser is destroyed. If the two are within a whisker of level, nobody dies; both bounce apart. Nothing else decides a fight, and the game teaches it without a word: on every clash it freezes for a beat and draws a line at each bird's height, so you can see exactly why it went the way it did.

Controls, and why holding feels different

On touch, hold the left or right half of the screen and the bird auto-flaps five times a second while drifting that way, which climbs steadily with one thumb. Deliberate taps flap faster, up to nearly nine a second, and each tap adds a sideways kick, so tapping is the sharper, more skilled option. On a keyboard, the arrows or A and D steer, space climbs, and holding a key behaves like holding the screen. A mouse works like touch: click and hold either half.

Three power-ups, three different economies

A star grants about seven seconds of invincibility, during which any contact destroys the enemy for 1000 points; it turns up roughly once every couple of waves and inverts the whole game while it lasts. A heart adds one life, with no cap. A mushroom makes you about a third larger with a proportionally higher crest, so clashes tilt your way, and it absorbs one losing clash instead of costing a life. It also expires where it sits and reappears somewhere else, so keeping the edge means crossing the arena to chase it.

Eggs are the clock

Every downed enemy drops an egg worth 250 points. In the first three waves eggs simply rot if you ignore them. From wave four they hatch into fresh enemies, faster the deeper you go, down to a floor of four seconds, and each egg keeps the hatch timer of the wave it fell in.

The ground keeps moving

There are twelve arena layouts, each also played mirrored, so 24 distinct grounds rotate through a run. The arena changes every three waves, and nothing repeats until wave 72.

Scoring, lives and what it keeps

A standard bird is worth 500, the fast red hunters 750, any kill while invincible 1000, and eggs 250. You start with three lives, earn a guaranteed extra every five waves, and hearts stack on top of that. The only thing the game stores is your best score, in your own browser. It is free, has no ads and needs no account, and once loaded it keeps running if the connection drops; only a reload needs the network back.

Touch: hold the left or right half of the screen to fly that way. Keyboard: left and right arrows or A and D to steer, space to climb. Any key starts. Every clash goes to the higher bird.

Where the original went

Flappy Bird was removed from both app stores in February 2014 by its own creator, Dong Nguyen, at the height of its success. His stated reason was that it had become too addictive. It stayed gone for a decade.

In January 2024 the trademark was terminated in a proceeding brought by a company called Gametech Holdings, and that September the new holders, operating as The Flappy Bird Foundation, announced a reboot under the old name. Nguyen has publicly said he has no involvement in it and sold nothing. The reboot is real and playable, first on Telegram and then on mobile through 2025, and it lives at flappybird.org.
